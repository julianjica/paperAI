\begin{abstract}
We study a firm whose manager has limited attention and must decide which decisions to delegate. Delegation economizes on attention but exposes the firm to misaligned subordinates. We show that the scope of delegation is governed by a cutoff that does not depend on the manager's attention capacity, so that additional attention improves how delegated tasks are run rather than how many tasks are kept, and we characterize when the firm should integrate or outsource a task.
\end{abstract}
