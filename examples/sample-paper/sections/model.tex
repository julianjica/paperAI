\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

A manager faces $n$ tasks indexed by $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$. Task $i$ has importance $v_i > 0$, ordered so that $v_1 \geq v_2 \geq \dots \geq v_n$. The manager has attention capacity $A > 0$. Attending to task $i$ costs one unit of attention and yields payoff $v_i$. Delegating task $i$ costs no attention and yields $(1 - b) v_i$, where $b \in (0,1)$ measures the subordinate's misalignment.

The manager chooses a set $K \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}$ of tasks to keep, subject to $|K| \leq A$. Her payoff is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:payoff}
\Pi(K) = \sum_{i \in K} v_i + (1 - b) \sum_{i \notin K} v_i .
\end{equation}

We also allow the manager to spend attention on improving alignment: by devoting $a \geq 0$ units of attention to monitoring, she reduces misalignment to $b(a) = b e^{-a}$. The attention constraint becomes $|K| + a \leq A$.
